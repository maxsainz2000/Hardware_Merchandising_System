0. What this card built
===============================================================================

docs/user-guide.md expanded from the P6-12 stub (account recovery only) into
the full task-first guide spec section 20 requires: Procurement, Inventory,
POS, Admin, and Super Admin operating procedures. Organized by task ("take a
sale", "close the day", "buy and receive goods") rather than by screen, per
the card's own instruction.

Every screen described was read from the live MainWindow.xaml of each client
before being written up - not transcribed from the spec or invented. That
reading surfaced a real, previously undocumented fact: several
role-permission-matrix policies (Products.Manage, Suppliers.Manage,
PurchaseReturns.Manage, PurchaseOrders.Close, SalesReturns.ApproveExceptional,
Reports.View, Configuration.Manage, Audit.Review, Receiving.Prepare) have no
consuming WPF screen in any of the three clients as of Phase 6 - some are
reachable only by a direct API call (documented in section 8, with routes and
policies cross-checked against the live controller source), and two
(Audit.Review, Receiving.Prepare) are registered policies with no consuming
endpoint at all. This is stated plainly in the document rather than
glossed over (plan.md section 6, ADR-016's own standard for what a claim may
not overstate).

src/tests/Merchandising.Tests.Unit/UserGuideDocumentationTests.vb (8 tests) -
the P2-12/P3-08/P4-13/P5-14/P6-09 drift-check shape applied to this document:
reads the live PaymentWording.xaml resource and cross-checks the quoted G-24
wording against it verbatim; reflects over ProductsController,
SuppliersController, PurchaseOrdersController, ReceivingController,
SalesReturnsController, SystemSettingsController, and ReportsController to
assert every route template and effective <Authorize> policy quoted in
section 8 matches the live source exactly; cross-checks the four system
setting keys quoted against SystemSettingRegistry.Keys's own constants; scans
every controller source file to assert PolicyRegistry.Names.AuditReview and
PolicyRegistry.Names.ReceivingPrepare are still unconsumed (so the day either
gets a real endpoint, this suite - not just the document - goes stale and
fails); and resolves every evidence path the document cites against disk.

===============================================================================

Per this repo's own standard (P5-12: "a wording test that has never fired is
exactly as decorative as any other"), the route/policy drift-check was
watched fail before being trusted. docs/user-guide.md section 8's quoted
close-order route was deliberately edited from:

    | ... | `POST /api/v1/purchase-orders/{id}/close` ... |

to:

    | ... | `POST /api/v1/purchase-orders/{id}/finish` ... |

5. What this document does NOT claim - stated honestly, not smoothed over
===============================================================================

Section 8 of docs/user-guide.md records, rather than hides, that eight
role-permission-matrix policies have no consuming WPF screen as of Phase 6:
Products.Manage, Suppliers.Manage, PurchaseReturns.Manage,
PurchaseOrders.Close, and SalesReturns.ApproveExceptional are reachable only
by a direct API call (documented with real routes and bodies); Reports.View
and Configuration.Manage likewise have no report or settings screen in any
client; and Audit.Review and Receiving.Prepare are registered policies with
no consuming endpoint of any kind - reviewing the audit log today requires a
direct database read on the host, which the document states rather than
implying a review screen exists. None of this is a Phase 6 exit criterion by
itself; all of it is `ui-specification.md`'s own named Phase 7 UI-refinement
scope, and is recorded here so it is not forgotten by the document's own
silence.
