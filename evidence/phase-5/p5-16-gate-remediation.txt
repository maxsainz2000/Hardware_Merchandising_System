1. FINDING 1 CLOSED - the access-key half now exists, and was watched fail
===============================================================================

Two assertions, at the two levels the claim is made at.

POSLayoutTests.NoScreenAuthorsAnAccessKey_SoTabTraversalIsTheWholeKeyboardStory
  Walks the POS window's LIVE LOGICAL TREE, per screen, for all five
  mechanisms WPF recognises as an access key - not just the obvious one,
  because checking only Button.Content would pass a window whose tab strip or
  labels carried mnemonics:
    1. an underscore mnemonic in a ContentControl's Content
    2. an underscore mnemonic in a HeaderedContentControl's Header (TabItem,
       GroupBox)
    3. a Label with a Target (an access key that forwards focus, even when
       its own text carries no underscore)
    4. an explicit AccessText element
    5. any InputBinding - a KeyBinding gesture such as Ctrl+S is a keyboard
       shortcut under another name
  A doubled underscore is WPF's escape for a literal underscore and is
  deliberately NOT read as a mnemonic.

UiSpecificationDocumentationTests.NoClientWindow_AuthorsAnAccessKey_MatchingSection4sStatedConvention
  Scans all three MainWindow.xaml files as source text for the same five
  mechanisms. This is the level docs/ui-specification.md section 4 makes the
  claim at - "not used anywhere in any of the three clients" - so it is
  asserted at that level rather than inferred from the POS one.

WATCHED FAIL (both, before being trusted).

  Sabotage A - two mnemonics in POS MainWindow.xaml: Content="_Sign in" on
  the sign-in button, and Header="C_heckout" on the tab strip.

    Failed NoScreenAuthorsAnAccessKey_SoTabTraversalIsTheWholeKeyboardStory
    Assert.IsEmpty failed. Expected collection of size 0. Actual: 2.
    [Sign in] Button.Content = "_Sign in" declares Alt+S.
    [C_heckout] TabItem.Header = "C_heckout" declares Alt+h.

  Both mechanisms fired, each naming the screen to open and the exact
  Alt+letter - a failure message a reader can act on without opening the
  XAML. Reverted; suite green.

  Sabotage B - one mnemonic in Inventory MainWindow.xaml line 97:
  Content="_Search".

    Failed NoClientWindow_AuthorsAnAccessKey_MatchingSection4sStatedConvention
    src\Merchandising.Inventory\MainWindow.xaml: Content="_Search"

  The cross-client scan caught a client the POS test cannot see. Reverted;
  suite green.

WHAT THIS ASSERTS, STATED NARROWLY. It asserts the CONVENTION, which is that
no client authors an Alt+letter mnemonic and every control is reached by
Tab/Shift+Tab and activated by Enter/Space. It does not assert that mnemonics
exist - none do, in any of the three clients, and section 4 has always said
so. That convention is what makes the TabIndex assertion a complete account
of the keyboard story rather than half of one; if a future screen adds a
mnemonic, both tests turn red and section 4 gets rewritten, which is the
intended signal rather than a false alarm.
